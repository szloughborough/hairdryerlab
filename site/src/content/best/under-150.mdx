## Why the C$120 line matters more than the C$150 line for hair dryers

**Our dataset does not show a cliff at C$150. It shows one at C$120.**

| Price band | Reviews | Average rating | Complaint rate (1–2★) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Under C$50** | 3,259 | 3.39★ | **30.3%** |
| **C$50–120** | 887 | 3.52★ | **29.0%** |
| **Over C$120** | 334 | **4.43★** | **9.6%** |

**Two things worth noticing.**

**First, the C$50–120 band is barely better than under C$50.** Moving from a C$40 dryer to an C$80 one did not change the complaint rate meaningfully in our data — 30.3% to 29.0%. That is a genuinely useful finding, because it means the middle of the market is not buying you what buyers assume it is.

**Second, the improvement is concentrated above C$120.** Complaint rate falls by roughly two thirds, and the average rating jumps from 3.5★ to 4.43★.

**How to use it.** If you are choosing between a dryer at about C$80 and one at about C$100, the C$100 option is closer to the band that actually performs differently — but the Slopehill Brushless at about C$80 carries a 4.6★ rating from 6,087 reviews, which is individual-product evidence that outweighs the band average.

**The honest limitation.** Price band and brand are correlated in our data, and we cannot fully separate them. We also cannot tell you whether the C$120 threshold is causal or whether it simply marks where better-engineered products start. What we can tell you is that it is the strongest single predictor we found across 8,261 reviews.

## What to check on arrival, whichever hair dryer you buy

**Two things, and both are quick.**

**1. The ALCI plug** — a rectangular GFCI-style box on the plug end with test and reset buttons. One Canadian reviewer flagged a dryer sold on Amazon.ca as **non-compliant and recalled because it lacked the mandatory ALCI plug**.

**2. The cord strain relief** where it enters the handle. Cord and connector failure accounted for **417 mentions** in our dataset, the second most common failure mode after complete stoppage at 601. Given that the **median reported failure across the category is 5.5 months**, this is not idle advice — see [how long hair dryers actually last](/guides/how-long-does-a-hair-dryer-last/).

**And one thing not to worry about:** the wattage figure. It measures power draw, not drying speed — our [wattage guide](/guides/how-many-watts/) explains why "2000W" tells you less than it appears to.
