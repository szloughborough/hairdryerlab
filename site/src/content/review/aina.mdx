## AINA hair dryer FAQ

**Is the AINA hair dryer any good?**
For about C$26 it is one of the better-structured budget options we track. It has 3,887 ratings at 4.4 stars, sells roughly 3,030 units a month at BSR #2, and — unusually at this price — the listing is brand-operated with a single seller held by Aina Canada Official Store.

**Why does the brand-operated listing matter on a cheap hair dryer?**
Because warranty handling is the second-largest complaint cluster in our dataset at 610 negative mentions and an 81% negative share, largely driven by listings with several competing resellers where nobody owns the claim. A single brand-held Buy Box gives you one counterparty. Both slopehill and Conair lack that structure.

**Is the AINA hair dryer dual voltage?**
It is not marketed as dual voltage, so treat it as a home dryer. Voltage was the single most-discussed topic in our entire dataset at 785 mentions. If you travel, the Dreame Pocket Pro or the Dyson Supersonic Travel are the options that work abroad.

**Does the AINA come with a diffuser?**
Yes, a diffuser is included, and at about C$26 it is one of the cheapest ways to get one. Attachment security is the most common curly-hair complaint in our data at 186 mentions and 57% negative, so check the fit when it arrives and return it inside the return window if it does not hold.

**Is the AINA hair dryer powerful enough for thick hair?**
It is a 1600W conventional design rather than a high-speed brushless one, so it relies more on heat than on airflow. Airflow complaints in our dataset run one way — reviewers say a dryer is too weak for their hair, essentially never too powerful. For dense hair we would look at the Laifen SE2 or the Slopehill Brushless.

**Who sells the AINA hair dryer in Canada?**
The listing is held by Aina Canada Official Store with a single seller and no competing resellers. That is the same structure Laifen, Dreame, wavytalk and Dyson use, and it is rare in the under-C$30 band where third-party resellers dominate.
