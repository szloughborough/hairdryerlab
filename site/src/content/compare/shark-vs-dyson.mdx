**What we cannot tell you.** We have not measured airflow or dry time on any of these units. The category's own marketing language — "high-velocity," "high-speed," "professional performance" — has no consistent unit attached to it by any brand, which is exactly why we do not repeat it as fact.

## Heat and scalp protection: Shark vs Dyson

**This is Shark's clearest feature advantage, and it maps onto a real complaint pattern in our data.**

- **Shark SpeedStyle Pro** is marketed with **Scalp Shield**, which limits air temperature near the scalp *(manufacturer claim)*
- **Dyson Supersonic Nural** takes a different route with heat sensing that adjusts to distance from the head *(manufacturer claim)*
- **Temperature and overheating generated 590 negative mentions in our dataset** — 54% of all heat-related comments were negative *(review analysis)*
- Reviewers describe dryers that "run too hot on high" and force constant use of the cool button, and in several cases, **burns to the scalp or wrist** *(buyer feedback)*

**The honest read.** Scalp protection targets a complaint that genuinely exists in our data. But the severe outcomes we captured — melting plastic, burns, smoke — were hardware failures rather than heat-setting problems. A scalp shield does not protect you from a cord that fails at the handle junction.

**For fine hair or a sensitive scalp**, this feature is the strongest single argument for choosing Shark over a plain high-speed dryer at the same price.

## Shark vs Dyson: FAQ

**Is Shark as good as Dyson?**
On features, Shark's SpeedStyle Pro is competitive — it adds scalp protection and a styling suite. On proven reliability, neither Shark nor Dyson is well documented in our dataset, and Shark has only 64 Canadian ratings against Dyson's 310 and 125. Dyson has the longer track record and the Canadian service network.

**Why is Shark cheaper than Dyson?**
In Canada the Shark SpeedStyle Pro is about C$200 against about C$400 for the Dyson Supersonic Travel and about C$630 for the Supersonic Nural. The gap is roughly 2× to 3.2× — narrower than the Laifen vs Dyson gap. You are paying less for brand equity and service network rather than for airflow.

**What is the difference between Shark FlexStyle and SpeedStyle?**
They are different product categories. FlexStyle is a multi-styler comparable to the Dyson Airwrap, and it carries the bulk of Shark's Canadian search volume. SpeedStyle Pro is a high-velocity hair dryer comparable to the Supersonic. Do not compare FlexStyle to a Supersonic — they do different jobs.

**How many reviews does the Shark SpeedStyle Pro have in Canada?**
Only 64 ratings on Amazon.ca as of 2026-10-02, against 310 for the Dyson Supersonic Travel and 125 for the Supersonic Nural. That is a small sample, and it is the single biggest limitation on any Shark recommendation — there are not yet enough long-term Canadian owners to judge reliability.

**Does Shark have a scalp protection feature Dyson does not?**
Yes. The SpeedStyle Pro is marketed with a Scalp Shield feature that limits air temperature near the scalp, which is relevant if you have a sensitive scalp or fine hair. Dyson's equivalent approach is heat sensing on the Supersonic Nural rather than a scalp-specific shield.

**Which is better for travel, Shark or Dyson?**
Dyson. The Supersonic Travel uses universal voltage, which makes it usable in Europe, the UK and Australia with only a plug adapter. The Shark SpeedStyle Pro is not marketed as dual voltage, so it is a home dryer rather than a travel dryer.

## Our verdict on Shark vs Dyson

**Shark has the better feature case; Dyson has the better evidence case.**

The Shark SpeedStyle Pro at about C$200 is genuinely interesting: it is half the price of a Supersonic, it currently outsells both Dyson models in Canada, and Scalp Shield addresses a complaint that appears 590 times in our review data. If you have a sensitive scalp or want a styling suite without Dyson pricing, it is a reasonable purchase.

What holds it back is not performance — it is that **64 Canadian ratings is not enough to tell you how long it lasts.** Dyson's C$200–430 premium buys a longer track record, a Canadian service network, and universal voltage on the Travel model, and those are concrete things rather than marketing.

**Our pick for value: the Shark SpeedStyle Pro.** **Our pick if you want the most evidence behind the decision: the Dyson Supersonic Travel.** And if you simply want fast high-speed drying for the least money, the **Laifen Air at about C$100** beats both on price per unit of airflow — see our [Laifen vs Dyson comparison](/comparisons/laifen-vs-dyson/).
