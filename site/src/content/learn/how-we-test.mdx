**Almost every hair dryer buying guide is written from the manufacturer's spec sheet. Ours is not.** We build our guides from two things we can actually verify: a large-scale analysis of verified Canadian buyer reviews, and current Amazon.ca listing data. We do not have completed bench measurements yet — and rather than imply otherwise, this page tells you exactly what we do, what we do not do, and how to judge each claim we make. It also sets out, in full, the test protocol each unit will run through, so that when a measured number finally appears here you can check how it was produced.

---

## Why we test hair dryers instead of repeating manufacturer specs

**We test because the specifications brands publish do not answer the question buyers actually have.** A listing tells you a wattage figure and calls the dryer "professional" or "powerful". It does not tell you whether the dryer will still work in month eight, how loud it is at your ear, or whether the diffuser stays on. Those are the things Canadian buyers write about after they have paid.

**What the 8,261 reviews we coded say about that gap.** The topics buyers raise most are not the topics listings lead with. Airflow and drying speed drew **1,585 mentions**, ease of use **1,579**, size and storage **1,429**, weight **1,318**, attachments **1,156**, and durability **1,120** — of which **84% were negative** *(review analysis)*. Heat and temperature drew **1,093 mentions with 54% negative**, and noise **848 with 33% negative** *(review analysis)*.

**The finding that decided our whole method.** Of the reviews that named a specific month of failure, **552 reviews produced a median reported failure time of 5.5 months and a mean of 6.0 months**, while only **0.8%** failed out of the box *(review analysis)*. This is a wear-out category, not a dead-on-arrival category — and no spec sheet describes it. That is the gap a test protocol is supposed to close.

**Our first bench round covers five models spanning the three price bands Canadian buyers actually shop in.** They were chosen so that the results are comparable across price, not so that a favourite wins.

**What we do not test, and why.** We do not test models we cannot buy in Canada through a Canadian listing, models with no verifiable Canadian review base, or models sold only by resellers we cannot identify. **No measurement or rating on this site is derived from a product supplied by a brand** — if a brand ever supplies a unit, we will say so on the page for that model.

## How we source the hair dryers we test

**We buy every test unit at retail with our own money.** Units are purchased from Amazon.ca as ordinary customer orders, at the normal listed price, using affiliate revenue and our own funds. A brand cannot send us a hand-picked unit, and no brand knows in advance which unit we will buy.

**What we record at purchase.** Order date, the exact variant and price paid, the seller holding the Buy Box, whether the listing is brand-operated or reseller-held, and whether fulfilment is FBA. Seller structure is not paperwork: with a **median reported failure of 5.5 months** *(review analysis)*, the warranty counterparty matters, and a listing that changes hands can leave a warranty claim with no stable party to make it against.

**Why retail sourcing is the point.** A unit that arrives from a retail order is a unit any reader could have received. That is the only version of a test result worth publishing.

## Test environment: the conditions every measurement is taken under

**Every measurement on this site will be taken in the same room, under the same conditions, and the conditions will be printed next to the number.** A measurement without its conditions is not reproducible, and a number you cannot reproduce is not evidence.

| Condition | How we fix it |
|---|---|
| Room | One indoor room in Canada, door closed, no other appliance running |
| Background noise | Measured and recorded immediately before each dryer is switched on, in dB @ 1 m |
| Power supply | Standard Canadian 120 V household outlet, no extension cord, no power bar |
| Unit run-in | Each unit run for 5 min 00 sec before measurement, so results reflect a warm dryer |
| Repeats | Every reading taken 3 times; the spread between repeats is published, not hidden |
| Ambient temperature | Recorded at the start of each session, in °C |
| Wet medium | One standard swatch per drying test, saturated to a fixed starting mass in g |

**Instruments.** A 0.1 g kitchen scale for mass, a handheld anemometer for airflow velocity, an infrared thermometer for surface temperature at distance, a sound-level app for pressure level, a steel tape for cord length in m, and a stopwatch. **Phone-based sound measurement carries an accuracy of roughly ±2 dB**, and we state that limitation every time we publish a noise figure rather than presenting an app reading as laboratory-grade.

**What we still need.** We must confirm no model enters the round without a Canadian ALCI plug inspection and a record of the shipped wattage rating in W. Until those are documented, a unit stays out of the published results.

## Drying-speed test: what we will measure, and why we have not measured it yet

**We have not measured drying speed on any model, and we will not publish an estimate.** Drying time is the specification buyers dispute most often, and the only honest response is a number we produce ourselves *(review analysis)*.

**Why this test matters more than the spec sheet.** Airflow and drying speed were the most-discussed topics in our dataset at **1,585 mentions**, and the complaints run in one direction — reviewers describe dryers that are too weak for their hair, almost never too powerful *(review analysis)*. Wattage does not resolve that, because wattage measures power draw rather than air movement.

**The protocol we will follow, per unit.**

| Step | Protocol |
|---|---|
| 1 | Saturate one standard swatch to a fixed starting mass, weighed in g |
| 2 | Remove excess water by centrifuge spin at a fixed speed and duration, then re-weigh in g |
| 3 | Run the dryer on its highest speed setting and highest heat setting |
| 4 | Hold the nozzle at a fixed distance from the swatch, recorded in m |
| 5 | Time to fully dry, recorded in min:sec, stopping when the swatch returns to its dry mass in g |
| 6 | Repeat 3 times; publish all three times and the spread |

**We will also record airflow velocity** at a fixed distance from the nozzle on each speed setting, in m/s, because air movement — not heat — is what carries water away. **Neither figure exists yet.**

## Noise test: the measurement we will publish in dB @ 1 m

**We have not measured noise on any model, so no decibel figure appears anywhere on this site.** When the first unit completes the protocol, the figure will be published as a sound pressure level in dB @ 1 m, with the background level recorded beside it.

**Why buyers ask about noise.** Noise appeared in **848 reviews in our dataset — 10.3% of the total — with 33% of mentions negative** *(review analysis)*. It is the fourth-largest concern behind airflow, ease of use and weight, and it is the one that decides whether a dryer is usable at 6am in an apartment.

**The protocol we will follow, per unit.**

| Step | Protocol |
|---|---|
| 1 | Measure and record background level in dB @ 1 m with the dryer off |
| 2 | Place the sound meter at 1 m from the dryer, at the height of a person using it |
| 3 | Measure each speed and heat setting, 3 times per setting |
| 4 | Publish the average of the repeats, the spread between them, and the background level |
| 5 | State the meter and its accuracy limitation on the same page as the figure |

**A decibel number without its distance and background is not comparable to anything**, which is why we publish all three together or none.

## Weight test: how we will report mass in grams

**We have not weighed any unit, and no gram figure on this site comes from our own bench.** Every weight figure we publish will come from our own scale, in g, with the configuration stated.

**Why weight is worth measuring carefully.** Weight drew **1,318 mentions in our dataset, with 73% of mentions positive** — buyers genuinely notice when a dryer is light *(review analysis)*. But "light" is not a measurement, and a bare number is not either: a dryer weighed without its cord is a different object from one weighed with it.

**The protocol we will follow, per unit.**

| Step | Protocol |
|---|---|
| 1 | Weigh the complete unit including the cord assembly, in g |
| 2 | Weigh the hand-held section alone, without the cord, in g |
| 3 | Weigh each attachment separately, in g, so the number for a diffuser is its own |
| 4 | Measure cord length from the point where it leaves the body to the plug, in m |
| 5 | Record every figure at 0.1 g resolution, and state which configuration each figure describes |

**We will not publish a single "weight" figure with no configuration attached**, because that is how a 400 g claim and a 550 g claim end up describing the same dryer.

## Heat test: surface temperature, airflow temperature and safety checks

**We have not measured the temperature of any unit, and we publish no °C figure from our own testing.** Heat is the highest-risk topic in our dataset, so it is the one we are least willing to estimate.

**Why heat is the safety dimension, not a performance footnote.** Heat and temperature drew **1,093 mentions with 54% negative** *(review analysis)*. The failure modes reviewers describe are specific: **smoke or fire in 182 mentions, 96% of them negative · heating element failure in 71 · melted or deformed plastic in 64** *(review analysis)*. A burning smell appeared in **215 reviews, 84% of them negative** *(review analysis)*.

**The protocol we will follow, per unit.**

| Step | Protocol |
|---|---|
| 1 | Run the unit on its highest heat setting for 5 minutes |
| 2 | Measure the grip surface temperature with an infrared thermometer, in °C |
| 3 | Measure the outlet temperature at a fixed distance from the nozzle, recorded in m |
| 4 | Measure the temperature at each heat step, and record the lowest step, in °C |
| 5 | Confirm the stated wattage against the rating label, in W |
| 6 | Inspect the plug end for a rectangular ALCI device with test and reset buttons — the Canadian requirement |
| 7 | Record any smell, noise change or housing deformation during the run, without interpreting it |

**The ALCI check is a Canadian specific.** It has no equivalent in most US coverage, and **785 mentions of voltage make it the single most-discussed topic in our entire dataset** *(review analysis)*. A dryer sold into Canada without compliant plug protection is a different product from one with it.

## Real-world use: the long-term reliability track our data demands

**We have not started the long-term track, and it is the part of this page we most want to finish.** Bench numbers describe a new unit on one afternoon. They cannot answer the question our dataset says buyers care about most: how long does it last?

**What we will do, and when we will publish.** Each of the five test units is logged with its serial number, then used on a fixed schedule that mimics real household use — once a day for a 10 min 00 sec session. We will log the date, running time, and any change in smell, sound, airflow or switch feel, and we will publish progress updates at **3 months, 6 months and 12 months**.

**Why a fixed schedule rather than ad-hoc testing.** Our dataset's **median reported failure of 5.5 months across 552 reviews** *(review analysis)* falls inside the first year of ownership, and **0.8% of failures were out of the box** *(review analysis)*. If the track is not scheduled, month eight arrives with nothing recorded. Real-world use also covers what a bench cannot: cord strain at the entry point, which drew **417 mentions in our dataset** and is the second most common failure mode *(review analysis)*.

## Hair-type feedback: who we ask, and how we label it

**Every result will be read by testers with different hair types, and their comments will be labelled as buyer-level feedback rather than as measurement.** Hair type changes which dryer works, and none of our bench figures can capture that.

**What the dataset already shows us.** Feedback splits by texture rather than by brand. Weight and size mentions skew positive — **73% of weight mentions and 74% of size and storage mentions** *(review analysis)* — while heat and temperature mentions run **54% negative** *(review analysis)*. Ionic claims have the best record in our data at **219 mentions with only 17% negative**, the most consistently positive feature we coded *(review analysis)*.

**How we will handle hair-type input, and how we will label it.**

| Source | How it appears on the site | What it can and cannot support |
|---|---|---|
| Structured test data | Published as **Measured**, with conditions | Supports a claim about the unit we tested |
| Testers with different hair types | Published as **Buyer feedback** | Illustrates an experience; it is not a measurement |
| Coded review patterns | Published as **Review analysis** | Supports a pattern across many buyers, not a claim about one unit |
| Brand statements | Published as **Manufacturer claim** | Unverified; we do not repeat it as fact |

**We are not hair stylists, and we do not claim to be.** We are an editorial team that codes reviews and runs a documented protocol. When a question is genuinely a professional one — whether a heat setting is safe for chemically treated hair, for example — we say it is outside what our method can settle rather than answering as if it were not.

## Price and value methodology: no composite scores, and no precise prices

**We do not publish a single number that scores a hair dryer, and we do not publish precise prices.** Both choices are deliberate and both cost us the format most buying guides use.

**Price, stated honestly.** Prices change weekly, and the site deliberately shows approximate Canadian dollar figures such as "about C$100" rather than an exact figure that may already be wrong. Where a number would be stale, we say to check the current price on the listing instead. The band thresholds we use are **C$50** and **C$120**.

**Why price band, and not price, is the value finding.** In our dataset, the complaint rate by band ran as follows *(review analysis)*:

| Price band | Reviews coded | Complaint rate |
|---|---|---|
| Under C$50 | 3,259 | 30.3% |
| C$50 to C$120 | 887 | 29.0% |
| Over C$120 | 334 | 9.6% |

**What that table does not mean.** It is not a claim that a specific expensive dryer is better, and it is not a recommendation to spend more than you need. It is a pattern across 4,480 coded reviews, and the cheapest band in it has roughly three times the complaint rate of the most expensive. **That pattern is the reason our recommendations discuss the band a model sits in, and why we say plainly when a model's price buys nothing measurable.**

**Why we refuse a total score.** A composite such as a single number out of ten would require us to decide how much noise is worth against weight, and to whom. A buyer who dries fine hair at 6am and a buyer drying thick hair on a Sunday morning need different things from the same dryer. A score hides that trade-off; a band, a failure mode and a warranty structure expose it.

## Affiliate independence: how the site is funded and what brands cannot buy

**Some links on this site are affiliate links.** If you buy a product through one of them, we may earn a commission from the retailer at no extra cost to you. That commission funds the retail purchase of our test units.

**What a brand cannot buy.** A ranking, a rating, a spot in a table, the removal of a criticism, or a place in the first bench round. Rankings come from the review analysis and the listing data described on this page. Our rankings have repeatedly favoured models with lower commission rates where the durability evidence supported them, and we have excluded models that sell well but carry a poor complaint profile.

**How we handle any future brand involvement.** If a brand ever pays for anything — a sponsored placement, a supplied unit, an event — it is labelled on the page where it appears and kept out of rankings. If a brand supplies a unit, the page for that model will say so, and it will not be counted in the bench round.

**Why we publish this rather than a trust badge.** Trust here comes from disclosure you can check, not from a seal we award ourselves. Full details are in our [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/), and our editorial rules are in our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/).

## Update policy: how corrections, changes and test results are dated

**We re-check prices and ratings monthly, and we date every check.** Each guide shows a "last updated" date, and comparison tables state the date the price and rating data was verified.

**What changes, and how it is shown.**

| Change | What appears on the page |
|---|---|
| A measurement completes | The figure is added with its conditions, and the page's "last updated" date changes |
| A price or rating moves | The table is refreshed and its verification date is updated |
| A factual error is found | The page is corrected and the "last updated" date changes rather than the edit happening silently |
| A model is discontinued or a listing structure changes | The entry is re-labelled, and it can leave the recommendations entirely |

**How to challenge us.** If you find a number in our guides that carries no source label, or a claim you believe is wrong, [contact us](/contact/) and tell us. An unlabelled number is a bug in our own rules, and we will fix it. **When a measured result finally replaces an unverified claim on any guide, it will happen on a dated update — you will be able to see that a claim moved from manufacturer claim to measured, and when.** The failure data behind these rules is set out in full in [how long hair dryers actually last](/guides/how-long-does-a-hair-dryer-last/), and the models we currently cover are listed in our [hair dryer reviews](/reviews/).

## How to read our evidence labels

**We label the source of every significant claim, on the page.** This is the single most useful thing you can use to judge us.

| Label | What it means | How much weight to give it |
|---|---|---|
| **Review analysis** | A pattern across all 8,261 reviews | Strong for trends; not proof about one unit |
| **Amazon.ca data** | Price, rating, rating count, seller structure, sales estimate | Verifiable — check it yourself today |
| **Buyer feedback** | A single quoted experience | Illustrative, not proof |
| **Manufacturer claim** | Stated by the brand, not verified by us | Treat as unverified |
| **Measured** | Our own bench data, with conditions stated | Strongest — but check our method |

**Our guides currently use four of these five labels. Measured is not one of them yet**, because no unit has completed the protocol described above. When one does, the result will be labelled and dated, and the guides that quote that model will be updated to say so.

## What we will not do

**These are hard rules, not aspirations.**

1. **We will not invent measurements.** No number appears on this site that we did not measure, and no specification is presented as our finding when it came from a brand. Where a number is not yet measured, we say so rather than estimate.
2. **We will not use other sites' ratings as our own.** We never generate a machine-readable aggregate rating from Amazon or retailer reviews. Their ratings are theirs; ours will be ours, and only after we test.
3. **We will not hide a weakness.** Every product we recommend gets a “what it does not do well” section, including our top pick.
4. **We will not print a price we cannot stand behind.** Prices appear as approximate Canadian figures; if we cannot confirm the current price for the exact variant, we say to check the current price on the listing instead of showing a stale number.
5. **We will not let a brand edit a conclusion.** Brands cannot pay for a ranking, a rating, or the removal of a criticism.
6. **We will not claim a product suits everyone.** Every guide says who should *not* buy it.
7. **We will not claim expertise we do not have.** We are an editorial team, not electricians, dermatologists or stylists, and we do not present ourselves as any of those.

## Frequently asked questions

**Have you physically tested the hair dryers you recommend?**
Not yet — no bench measurement is complete. Our current guides rest on analysis of 8,261 verified Canadian reviews plus current Amazon.ca listing data. Bench testing is in progress, and each measured result will be published with its date and conditions.

**How many reviews do you analyse?**
8,261 Amazon.ca reviews covering 98 hair dryer models, spanning February 2015 to September 2026. 93.1% were verified purchases. We code each review against 20 concern categories and split them by star rating.

**Why do your review-based averages differ from the star rating on Amazon?**
Our sample averages 3.37 stars while the same products display 4.3–4.6 stars on Amazon.ca. The export skews toward recent and critical reviews. We therefore use it for theme and pain-point patterns only, and we quote the Amazon rating when stating a product's rating.

**How do you make money, and does it affect your rankings?**
Some links on this site are affiliate links and we may earn a commission if you buy through them, at no extra cost to you. Rankings are set by the review analysis and listing data described on this page. Brands cannot pay to change a ranking or remove a criticism.

**How often do you update your guides?**
We re-check prices and ratings monthly and show the date of the last check on every guide. When a correction is needed we update the page and change the "last updated" date rather than quietly editing it.
