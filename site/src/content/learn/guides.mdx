## Answers we can give you now

**Rather than leave you with nothing while those are written, here are the short versions with the evidence attached.**

### Why hair dryers fail

Across **8,261 Canadian reviews**, the median reported failure time was **5.5 months** and the mean 6.0 months. Only **0.8%** failed out of the box, so this is a wear-out problem, not a bad batch.

| Failure mode | Mentions |
|---|---|
| Stopped working entirely | 601 |
| **Cord / connector failure** | 417 |
| Switch or button failure | 300 |
| Motor / airflow degradation | 196 |
| Smoking or catching fire | 182 |

**The practical takeaway:** check the **cord strain relief** where it enters the handle on arrival. It is the second most common failure point and it is visible.

### Why your dryer smells like burning

**215 reviews mention burning or plastic smell, and 84% of them are negative.** In several cases a burning smell preceded a heating element failure within months. **If your dryer smells like burning, stop using it** and contact the seller or brand.

### What wattage does and does not tell you

Wattage describes **power draw**, not drying speed. A modern high-speed brushless dryer can move more air than a 1875W conventional dryer while drawing less. This is why our guides rank on **airflow evidence** rather than wattage, and why "2000W" in a product title tells you less than it appears to.

### Voltage, in one table

| Situation | What you need |
|---|---|
| Using a Canadian dryer at home | 120V, ALCI plug — standard |
| Taking it to Europe, UK or Australia | **Dual voltage 110–220V** and a plug adapter |
| Taking it to the United States | Nothing — same 120V, same plug |
| Using a US-purchased dryer in Canada | It works, but warranty service may mean shipping to the US |

**Voltage was the single most-discussed topic in our dataset at 785 mentions** — ahead of noise, weight and price. It is the most common travel mistake in this category.

### What actually matters when comparing two dryers

| Matters | Does not reliably matter |
|---|---|
| Airflow evidence | Advertised ion count |
| Heat control range | "Professional" as a label |
| Measured weight | Peak wattage figure |
| Attachment security | Number of attachments included |
| Seller structure and warranty | Colourway range |

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between ionic and ceramic hair dryers?**
They are not alternatives. Ceramic describes the heating element, which heats more evenly than a bare metal coil. Ionic describes a generator that emits negative ions to reduce static. Many dryers use both, which is why treating them as competing categories is a mistake.

**Does higher wattage mean a better hair dryer?**
Not on its own. Wattage describes power draw, not drying speed. A modern high-speed brushless dryer can move more air than a 1875W conventional dryer while drawing less power, which is why our guides rank on airflow evidence rather than on wattage claims.

**Why does my hair dryer smell like burning?**
A persistent burning smell is the most common precursor reviewers describe before a dryer dies. It appeared in 215 reviews in our dataset with 84% of them negative, and in several cases a burnt smell preceded a heating element failure. Stop using it and contact the seller or brand.

**Can I use my Canadian hair dryer in Europe?**
Only if it is dual voltage. Most Canadian dryers are 120V only, and plugging one into a 220–240V outlet will damage it, sometimes immediately. Use a plug adapter only where the dryer supports the local voltage; otherwise you need a dual-voltage model.

**Why do hair dryers break so often?**
Across 8,261 Canadian reviews the median reported failure was 5.5 months, and only 0.8% failed out of the box — so this is a wear-out problem rather than a defective-batch problem. The most common failure modes were complete stoppage at 601 mentions and cord or connector failure at 417.
