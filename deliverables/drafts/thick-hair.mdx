## What actually matters when drying thick hair

**Drying thick hair is a water-removal problem, and the amount of water scales with hair mass.**

That reframes what the dryer needs to do. It is not primarily a heater — it is a **pump for moving air across a wet surface**. More air at a lower temperature removes water faster than more heat does, because heat alone does not move water off the hair; it only raises the temperature of the water that is already there.

**This is why high-speed brushless dryers changed the category.** They move a much larger volume of air for the same or lower power draw than a conventional element dryer. Our data reflects that: the complaints are about weakness, not about power.

**The specific failure of using high heat on dense hair** is worth spelling out. Heat applied to thick hair dries and seals the outer layer while the interior stays damp. So you get a dry-feeling surface, heat damage to the cuticle, and you still need more drying time — the worst of both outcomes. Heat and temperature generated **1,093 mentions with 54% negative**, and this is the pattern behind them.

**Two ergonomic factors matter more for you than for most buyers.** Thick hair means longer sessions, so **weight** matters: 1,318 mentions with **73% positive**. And because you are working through sections, **control placement** matters: usability is the second-largest topic in our data at **1,579 mentions with 39% negative**, and reviewers describe buttons they cannot find without looking *(review analysis)*.

**What we cannot tell you yet:** how many cubic metres per minute any of these dryers actually moves. We have not measured it. Every comparison table row for measured airflow on this site reads "not yet tested" rather than carrying an estimate, and that is deliberate.

## What does not matter for thick hair

**Three things this category markets hardest for dense hair, and none of them holds up.**

**1. Peak wattage.** Wattage describes power draw, not drying speed. A high-speed brushless dryer can move more air than a 1875W conventional dryer while drawing less. "2000W" in a product title tells you less than it appears to — and our complaint data is about weakness, which wattage does not measure.

**2. Ion counts.** Ionic as a feature has the best record in our data at **17% negative**, but that is a frizz benefit, not a drying-speed one. The count itself has no consistent definition across brands.

**3. "Professional" or "salon."** Neither has a regulatory or industry definition in this category. They appear on dryers from C$25 to C$630 in our dataset.

**What does matter and gets under-marketed:** the low heat setting, the weight, and where the controls sit. All three matter more for long sessions on dense hair than any headline number.

## How to dry thick hair without a 30-minute session

**The method changes the outcome more than the dryer does.**

1. **Remove as much water as possible before switching on.** Towel or microfibre blot thoroughly. The first minutes of drying soaking hair are the least efficient of the whole session
2. **Section the hair first.** Four to six clips. Dense hair dried in one mass takes far longer than the same hair dried in sections
3. **Start at the roots and scalp, then work out.** The scalp area holds the most water and is the slowest to dry
4. **Use maximum airflow but medium heat.** This is the core principle — more air, less heat
5. **Keep the dryer moving.** Holding it still concentrates heat on the surface while the interior stays damp
6. **Do not seal the surface early with high heat.** Once the outer layer dries and tightens, the interior takes even longer
7. **Finish with the cool shot.** It sets the result and it is the step most people skip

**One expectation to set:** thick hair takes longer. That is a property of how much water is in it, not a sign your dryer is broken. The question is whether you are removing that water efficiently — which is airflow and sectioning, not heat.

**Why weight matters here specifically.** If your session is 20 minutes rather than 8, the difference between a 400 g and a 600 g dryer is the difference between finishing comfortably and setting it down. Weight was the third most-discussed topic in our dataset at **1,318 mentions and 73% positive** *(review analysis)*.

## What to avoid with thick hair

- **Reaching for maximum heat.** It dries the surface, damages the cuticle, and leaves the interior damp
- **Drying the whole head in one section.** Sectioning is the single biggest time saver
- **Buying on wattage.** It does not measure what you need
- **A heavy dryer.** Long sessions make weight a real factor, not a comfort detail
