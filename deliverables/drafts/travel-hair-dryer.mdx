# Best Travel Hair Dryer

**Voltage is the entire decision, and most buying guides skip it.** Most hair dryers sold in Canada are **120V only**, and plugging one into a European 220–240V outlet will damage it — sometimes immediately. A plug adapter changes the shape of the plug, not the voltage, so it cannot solve the problem. **Voltage was the single most-discussed topic in our entire review dataset at 785 mentions** — ahead of noise, weight and price *(review analysis)*. That tells you it is the most common expensive mistake in this category. **Our pick:** the **Dreame Pocket Pro at about C$160**, which is dual voltage and carries the joint-highest rating above C$120 in our dataset at **4.6★**. If you want the longest-established option, the **Dyson Supersonic Travel at about C$400** uses universal voltage.

## The short answer on voltage

| Situation | What you need | What happens if you get it wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Using a dryer at home in Canada | 120V, ALCI plug — standard | — |
| Europe, UK, Australia (220–240V) | **Dual voltage 110–220V** + plug adapter | A 120V dryer is damaged, sometimes immediately |
| United States | Nothing — same 120V, same plug | — |
| Japan (100V) | Dual voltage, and expect reduced power | Underperformance rather than damage |
| Cruise ships | Check the line voltage; many are 110V | Varies by ship |

**Three terms to read carefully on a listing:**

- **"Dual voltage"** — runs on both 120V and 220–240V. Some switch automatically, some have a manual selector
- **"Universal voltage"** — same practical meaning, used by Dyson
- **"Compact"** or **"travel size"** — **only describes size.** It does not mean dual voltage

**That third one is where buyers get caught.** In our dataset, several listings use "compact" and "travel" interchangeably without stating a voltage range. If a listing does not state dual or universal voltage explicitly, assume it is 120V only.

## What a plug adapter cannot fix

**This is the single most expensive misunderstanding in this category, so it is worth stating precisely.**

**A plug adapter changes the physical shape of the connection.** It lets a North American plug fit a European socket. It does nothing to the electricity.

**A voltage converter changes the electricity.** It steps 220–240V down to 120V, or up. Hair dryers draw high wattage — often 1,600W to 1,875W — so a converter has to be rated comfortably above that. Cheap travel converters rated at 50W or 100W are intended for phone chargers and will fail, sometimes dangerously, on a hair dryer.

**The practical conclusion:** buying a dual-voltage dryer is almost always cheaper and safer than buying a converter capable of running a single-voltage dryer. For the price of a suitable converter you can buy the Revlon travel dryer outright.

**What about hotel-provided dryers?** They exist, and they are usually weak. If you are travelling with curly hair and need a diffuser, a hotel dryer will not have one — which is an argument for the Dreame Pocket Pro, since it includes a diffuser and dual voltage in one unit.

## Two safety checks before you travel

**Both of these come out of our review data, and both are quick.**

**1. Check the ALCI plug on arrival at home, not at the airport.** ALCI plugs provide shock protection and are the North American standard for hair dryers. One Canadian reviewer flagged a dryer sold on Amazon.ca as **non-compliant and recalled because it lacked the mandatory ALCI plug**. Look for the rectangular GFCI-style box with test and reset buttons on the plug end.

**2. Check the cord strain relief.** Cord and connector failure accounted for **417 mentions** in our dataset, the second most common failure mode after complete stoppage at 601. A dryer that gets packed and unpacked is more exposed to that stress than one that stays on a shelf. See [how long hair dryers actually last](/guides/how-long-does-a-hair-dryer-last/).

**One thing not to worry about:** the dryer's weight. Size and storage was the most *positively* discussed topic in our entire dataset at 1,429 mentions and 74% positive — buyers notice when a travel dryer is genuinely packable.

For a single-voltage dryer you would need a step-up or step-down converter, and hair dryers draw high wattage, so the converter has to be rated well above the dryer's draw.
