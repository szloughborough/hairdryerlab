# Shark Hair Dryer Review

**Read this first, because it is where most Shark shoppers go wrong: "Shark hair dryer" is two different products.** The **FlexStyle** is a multi-styler comparable to the Dyson Airwrap. The **SpeedStyle Pro** is a high-velocity hair dryer comparable to the Supersonic. They do different jobs, and comparing a FlexStyle to a Supersonic is a category error that a lot of buying content makes.

**This review covers the SpeedStyle Pro**, because that is the hair dryer. At **about C$200 with a 4.4★ rating from 64 Canadian ratings and BSR #13**, it currently **outsells both Dyson Supersonic models in Canada** *(Amazon.ca data)*. It also adds **Scalp Shield**, which limits air temperature near the scalp — a feature Dyson does not offer.

**The honest limitation: 64 ratings.** That is a small sample, and it is the single biggest constraint on this review.

## What we liked about the Shark SpeedStyle Pro

**1. Scalp Shield targets a complaint that genuinely exists in our data.**
Temperature and overheating generated **590 negative mentions**, 54% of all heat-related comments *(review analysis)*. Reviewers describe dryers that run too hot on high and force constant use of the cool button, and in several cases **burns to the scalp or wrist** *(buyer feedback)*. A feature that limits air temperature near the scalp addresses exactly that.

**2. It currently outsells both Dyson Supersonics in Canada.**
At **BSR #13** and roughly **288 units a month**, it sits ahead of the Supersonic Travel at #34 and the Nural at #36 *(Amazon.ca data)*. Recent momentum is a real signal, even if it is a different signal from long-term reliability.

**3. Shark holds the Buy Box.**
Two sellers compete, but the **Buy Box is held by Shark itself**, so the brand is the warranty counterparty. Warranty and service complaints are the second-largest cluster in our data at **610 negative mentions and 81% negative**, largely driven by reseller listings where nobody owns the claim. This listing is not that *(Amazon.ca data, review analysis)*.

**4. It includes a styling and drying suite rather than being a plain dryer.**
If styling is part of your routine, you are getting one device rather than a dryer plus a separate styler *(manufacturer claim)*.

**5. It sits in the price band where complaints fall sharply.**
Over C$120, our data shows a **9.6% complaint rate** across 334 reviews, against **30.3%** under C$50 across 3,259 *(review analysis)*.

## What we didn't like about the Shark SpeedStyle Pro

**1. Only 64 Canadian ratings.**
This is the whole caveat. The Dyson Supersonic Travel has **310** and the Laifen SE Lite has **550**. A 4.4★ average from 64 buyers is far less informative than the same average from 310, and we would not draw a reliability conclusion from it in either direction *(Amazon.ca data)*.

**2. Not dual voltage.**
It is a home dryer. Voltage was the single most-discussed topic in our entire dataset at **785 mentions**, and the Shark is on the wrong side of that for travel *(review analysis)*.

**3. about C$200 is real money for a thin evidence base.**
It is **2× the Laifen Air** and **1.4× the Laifen SE2**, both of which have larger Canadian review bases *(Amazon.ca data)*.

**4. Airflow claims are unmeasured by us.**
Shark markets it as "high-velocity," Dyson as "high-speed digital," Laifen as high-speed brushless. None of them attaches a unit. We have not measured it either *(our test protocol)*.

**5. It is a dryer, not a multi-styler — and the marketing blurs that.**
If you wanted FlexStyle, this is the wrong purchase. The naming is close enough that buyers mix them up, which is why we put the distinction at the top of this page.

## Shark SpeedStyle Pro performance and heat

**What we can say.** The SpeedStyle Pro is a high-velocity dryer, which means it moves air rather than relying on a hot element. That is the same fundamental approach Dyson and Laifen describe, and it is the right one for dense or long hair *(manufacturer claim)*.

**Why the approach matters.** Airflow was the most-discussed topic in our dataset at **1,585 mentions**, and the complaint pattern runs one way — reviewers rarely complain a dryer is too powerful, they complain it is too weak for their hair. Underpowered dryers are the most common single reason reviewers describe returning one *(review analysis)*.

**What Scalp Shield adds.** Dyson's comparable feature is heat sensing on the Supersonic Nural, which adjusts to distance from the head. Shark's approach is a shield that limits temperature near the scalp specifically. For **fine hair or a sensitive scalp**, that is the strongest single argument for choosing Shark over a plain high-speed dryer at the same price. See our [fine hair guide](/hair-types/fine-hair/).

**What we cannot say.** How fast it dries, how hot it runs, or how loud it is. We have not measured any of those, and every comparison table row for measured values on this site reads "not yet tested" rather than carrying an estimate.

## Shark hair dryer FAQ

**Is the Shark hair dryer as good as Dyson?**
On features, the SpeedStyle Pro is competitive — it adds Scalp Shield at roughly half the price of a Supersonic. On evidence, Dyson has the larger Canadian review base at 310 against 64, a longer product history and a Canadian service network. Shark has the price and one feature advantage.

**What is the difference between Shark FlexStyle and SpeedStyle?**
They are different product categories. FlexStyle is a multi-styler comparable to the Dyson Airwrap. SpeedStyle Pro is a high-velocity hair dryer comparable to the Supersonic. This review covers SpeedStyle Pro because it is the hair dryer — comparing FlexStyle to a Supersonic is comparing two different jobs.

**How many reviews does the Shark SpeedStyle Pro have in Canada?**
Only 64 ratings on Amazon.ca as of 2026-10-02. That is a small sample, and it is the single biggest limitation on this review. There are not yet enough long-term Canadian owners to judge reliability, and we will not pretend otherwise.

**Does the Shark SpeedStyle Pro have a scalp protection feature?**
Yes. It is marketed with Scalp Shield, which limits air temperature near the scalp. That directly targets the complaint fine-haired reviewers describe, which is heat rather than a lack of power. Dyson's equivalent approach is heat sensing on the Supersonic Nural.

**Is the Shark SpeedStyle Pro dual voltage?**
It is not marketed as dual voltage, so treat it as a home dryer rather than a travel dryer. Voltage was the single most-discussed topic in our entire dataset at 785 mentions. If you travel, the Dyson Supersonic Travel or the Dreame Pocket Pro are the options that work abroad.

**Is Shark sold by the brand on Amazon.ca?**
Partly. The SpeedStyle Pro listing has two sellers with the Buy Box held by Shark itself, so the brand is the counterparty. That is a better structure than a listing with five competing resellers and a third-party Buy Box.

## Our verdict on the Shark SpeedStyle Pro

**Reasonable value on features; thin on evidence.** The SpeedStyle Pro at about C$200 gives you Scalp Shield, a styling suite and a brand-held Buy Box, and it currently outsells both Dyson Supersonics in Canada. If you have a sensitive scalp or want a styling suite without Dyson pricing, it is a defensible purchase.

**What holds it back is not performance — it is that 64 Canadian ratings is not enough to tell you how long it lasts.** Dyson's C$200–430 premium buys a longer track record, a Canadian service network, and universal voltage on the Travel model, and those are concrete things rather than marketing.

**And check which Shark you are buying.** If you wanted the **FlexStyle**, that is a multi-styler and none of this page applies to it. If you want fast high-speed drying for the least money, the **Laifen Air at about C$100** beats the SpeedStyle on price per unit of airflow — with a larger Canadian review base as well.
