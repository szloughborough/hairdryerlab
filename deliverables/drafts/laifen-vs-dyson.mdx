# Laifen vs Dyson Hair Dryer

**The short answer:** Dyson builds the better dryer. **Laifen is the better buy for most Canadians.** The gap that matters is not performance — both move high airflow at lower heat — it is price and proven reliability. In Canada a Dyson Supersonic starts at **about C$400** against **about C$100 for the Laifen Air**, roughly a 4× gap. Our data does not show Laifen is unreliable; it shows Laifen's reliability is *unproven*, because the brand is young. Whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on how much a failure at month six would cost you.

## Price in Canada: Laifen vs Dyson

**The Laifen Air costs about C$100 and the cheapest Dyson Supersonic costs about C$400 — a 4× gap. The Supersonic Nural is 6.3×.**

**What you are paying for with Dyson.** Not airflow. Both brands describe the same fundamental approach: a high-speed motor moving a large volume of air at a lower temperature, rather than a hot element blasting heat. The Dyson premium buys brand equity, a genuine engineering reputation, a Canadian service network, and the confidence of a product family that has been on sale for a decade.

**What you are paying for with Laifen.** About a quarter of the price for the same core approach, in a package rated **4.5 stars from 278 Canadian buyers**. Laifen's contribution to this category was making high-speed drying accessible rather than aspirational.

**A practical way to think about it:**

| If you… | Buy |
|---|---|
| Want the lowest risk of an unresolved failure | Dyson |
| Want the best drying experience per dollar | Laifen |
| Travel internationally and need universal voltage | Dyson Supersonic Travel |
| Are replacing a C$30 dryer and want a real upgrade | Laifen Air |
| Have thick or long hair and a flexible budget | Laifen SE2, or Dyson Supersonic |

## Drying performance: Laifen vs Dyson

**Both brands use the same underlying approach, and that approach is the right one for most hair.**

- **High airflow at lower heat**, rather than a hot element doing the work *(manufacturer claim, both brands)*
- **Airflow was the most-discussed topic in our entire dataset at 1,585 mentions** — more than noise, weight, price or durability in raw mention volume *(review analysis)*
- The complaint pattern in that topic is one-directional: reviewers rarely complain a dryer is too powerful; they complain it is **too weak for their hair** *(review analysis)*
- Underpowered dryers are the most common single reason reviewers describe returning a dryer *(review analysis)*

**What we cannot tell you.** We have not measured airflow or dry time on any of these four models. Any specific second-count you read in a comparison like this one is either measured by someone with a bench, or invented. We will publish measured numbers on our [Lab Test Card](/how-we-test/) as each unit completes our protocol.

**Practical guidance:** if drying speed is your priority and your hair is thick or long, buy the higher-airflow model in either family — the Laifen SE2 at about C$145 or the Dyson Supersonic at about C$400.

## Weight and handling: Laifen vs Dyson

**Weight is the third most-discussed topic in our dataset at 1,318 mentions, and 73% of that discussion is positive — buyers notice when a dryer is light.**

- **Dyson Supersonic Travel** is designed as a lighter, more compact variant of the Supersonic *(manufacturer claim)*
- **Laifen Air** is positioned as "ultra light" in its listing title *(manufacturer claim)*
- The Laifen SE2 is heavier than the Air, and reviewers of step-up models across the category frequently note the extra weight *(review analysis)*

**Why this matters more than it sounds.** A hair dryer is held at arm's length for 5–15 minutes. Weight complaints in our dataset cluster around longer hair and longer sessions. If you dry your hair daily and take more than ten minutes, weight should be a higher priority in your decision than peak airflow.

**One number worth having:** a 100 g difference at arm's length feels meaningfully heavier over a ten-minute session. We will publish measured weight with cord, and the hand-held section separately, for all four models.

## Heat control: Laifen vs Dyson

**Heat and safety issues were the second-largest complaint cluster in our dataset, with 590 negative mentions on temperature and overheating.**

- **Dyson Supersonic Nural** adds heat sensing, which is the most advanced heat-control approach in this comparison *(manufacturer claim)*
- **Both Laifen models** use smart temperature control rather than fixed heat steps *(manufacturer claim)*
- **182 mentions of smoking or catching fire appear in our dataset, 96% of them negative reviews.** This is the most reliably negative signal we found *(review analysis)*
- **215 mentions of burning or plastic smell, 84% negative.** A persistent burning smell is the most common precursor reviewers describe before a dryer dies *(review analysis)*

**The honest read:** heat-control technology is a genuine differentiator, and it is one of the areas where Dyson's engineering investment shows. But the failures our data captures are mostly hardware — cord, switch, element — not control logic. Better heat control does not protect you from a cord that fails at the handle junction.

**For fine or thin hair**, heat control matters more than airflow, because the failure mode reviewers describe is scorching rather than slow drying *(review analysis)*.

## Laifen vs Dyson: FAQ

**Is Laifen as good as Dyson?**
On airflow approach and features it is close. On build quality and long-term reliability Dyson is ahead, and it has a decade of service history Laifen does not. The price gap is roughly 4×, and that gap is the entire question — our data does not show Laifen is unreliable, only that its reliability is unproven.

**Why is Dyson so much more expensive than Laifen?**
In Canada the Dyson Supersonic Travel is about C$400 and the Supersonic Nural is about C$630, against about C$100 for the Laifen Air. Dyson carries a decade of brand equity, a genuine engineering reputation and a Canadian service network. Whether that is worth 4× depends on how you weigh the risk of a failure at month six.

**Which is better for thick or long hair, Laifen or Dyson?**
Both move high airflow at lower heat, which is the right approach for dense hair. Laifen's SE2 at about C$145 is tuned for buyers who return dryers for being underpowered. Airflow was the most-discussed topic in our dataset at 1,585 mentions, so if drying speed is your priority, neither will disappoint.

**Does Laifen break more often than Dyson?**
We cannot say, and anyone who claims otherwise without long-term data is guessing. What we can say is that across 8,261 Canadian reviews the median reported failure was 5.5 months, and that dryers over C$120 had a 9.6% complaint rate against 30.3% under C$50. Laifen sits in the better band.

**Should I buy Laifen or save for Dyson?**
If a failure at month six would be a serious financial annoyance, buy Dyson — you are paying for a service network. If you are comfortable treating a C$100 dryer as a two-to-three year consumable, Laifen gives you roughly the same drying experience for a quarter of the price.

**Is Laifen a real Dyson alternative or just a copy?**
It is a genuine brushless high-speed dryer with its own engineering, not a clone. Both use high airflow at lower heat rather than blasting hot air. Laifen's weakness is service history and Canadian parts availability, not the technology.

## Our verdict on Laifen vs Dyson

**Dyson is the better dryer. Laifen is the better buy for most Canadians.**

That is not a contradiction. Dyson's advantages are real: a decade of engineering, a Canadian service network, universal voltage on the Travel model, and the Nural's heat sensing. If a failure at month six would be a genuine problem for you, the C$300 premium buys you an answer.

But the performance gap is narrower than the price gap. Both brands describe the same high-airflow, lower-heat approach, and both sit in the price band where our data shows complaint rates falling to **9.6%** from 30.3%.

**Our pick for value: the Laifen Air at about C$100.** **Our pick if reliability outweighs price: the Dyson Supersonic.**
