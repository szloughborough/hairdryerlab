## What actually matters when drying frizzy hair

**Frizz is what happens when the hair's outer layer is roughened and lifted, and two things in this category cause it: heat and mechanical disturbance.**

**Heat is the bigger one in our data.** Of 4,111 frizz mentions, the negative sentiment is not about airflow velocity or ion counts — it is about hair becoming **dry, brittle and flyaway after repeated high-heat use** *(review analysis)*. That is consistent with the heat cluster as a whole, which runs at **1,093 mentions and 54% negative**.

**Which is why the right approach is high airflow at lower heat**, the same approach Laifen, Dreame and Dyson all describe. You move more air so you need less heat for the same drying time. The element does less work and the hair experiences a lower peak temperature.

**Ionic technology is the second factor, and it is the one claim in this category worth seeking out.** Ion generators emit negative ions that neutralise the static charge that makes hair stand apart. Our data shows ionic mentions running at **219 with only 17% negative** — a dramatically better ratio than any other feature we coded, including airflow at 35% negative and heat control features at 54% *(review analysis)*.

**What we cannot support is the number.** Listings advertise specific ion counts, and there is no consistent definition of what those numbers mean across brands. We would treat the count as marketing and the feature as real.

**The third factor is mechanical.** Strong airflow on textured hair tangles it, and handling during drying creates frizz regardless of temperature. This is why a diffuser matters if your hair has any wave at all — our [diffuser guide](/guides/diffuser/) covers the mechanics.

## What does not matter for frizzy hair

**Three things this category markets hardest that our data does not support.**

**1. Ion counts.** Ionic as a feature has the best record in our data at 17% negative. The *number* of ions in a product title has no consistent definition across brands, and we would treat it as marketing. Two dryers both claiming "ionic" but advertising different counts are not necessarily different in any way you can verify.

**2. Wattage.** Frizz data points to heat as the mechanism, and higher wattage means more heat available per second. A 2000W dryer is not better for frizz than an 1800W one.

**3. "Anti-frizz" as a headline claim.** It appears at every price from C$25 to C$630 in our dataset. It is a marketing category, not a specification, and we cannot find a consistent meaning for it.

**What does matter and gets under-marketed:** the number and spacing of heat settings, and whether the lowest heat setting is genuinely cool. Neither appears in a typical listing headline.

## How to dry frizzy hair without making it worse

**The method matters as much as the dryer, and it costs nothing to change.**

1. **Start from damp, not soaking.** Blot with a microfibre towel. Drying soaking hair means more heat exposure for the same result
2. **Apply product before drying.** Frizz control is set during drying and cannot be repaired afterwards
3. **Use the lowest heat that works.** This is the single highest-value change, and our heat data explains why
4. **Keep the dryer moving, and keep it 15–20 cm away.** Holding it still concentrates heat on one spot
5. **Aim down the hair shaft.** Aiming against the cuticle lifts it, which is mechanically what frizz is
6. **Finish with the cool shot.** This is what sets the result, and it is the step most people skip
7. **Do not touch the hair until it is fully dry.** Handling while drying is the second mechanism behind frizz after heat
8. **If your hair has any wave, use a diffuser.** A concentrator on textured hair blows the pattern apart

**One thing worth knowing about the cool shot:** it works because cooling sets the hydrogen bonds in the hair in the shape they dried in. Skipping it means the style collapses as the hair cools on its own.

## What to avoid with frizzy hair

- **Highest heat to save time.** This is the mechanism our frizz data identifies, and it is the most common mistake
- **A concentrator nozzle on wavy or curly hair.** It focuses fast air and breaks the pattern apart
- **Buying on ion count.** The feature is real; the number is not verifiable
- **A dryer with only two heat settings.** You need a genuinely low step, not a "low" label
- **Touching or brushing while drying.** Mechanical disturbance creates frizz regardless of temperature
- **Ignoring the seller.** With a median reported failure of **5.5 months**, who honours the warranty matters more than most specifications

**Should I dry my hair on cold to prevent frizz?**
